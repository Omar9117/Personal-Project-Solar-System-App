GET TO KNOW THE PLANET

Explore Saturn

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our Solar System, after Jupiter. Like Jupiter, it is a gas giant with no solid surface that spacecraft can land on.

Its most remarkable feature is its ring system, which consists of many individual rings made mainly of water ice, along with rocky particles and dust. These rings stretch thousands of kilometres across space.

Saturn has a large family of moons. Some are scientifically fascinating, including Titan, which has lakes and rivers of liquid methane and ethane, and Enceladus, which releases water-rich plumes from its icy surface.

Did you know?

Saturn has an average density lower than water. This is because it is made mostly of lightweight elements, especially hydrogen and helium.

Saturn's rings are made mostly of water ice, with rocky material and dust.

WHAT IS INSIDE SATURN?

Internal Structure

Saturn has no solid outer surface. Its interior consists of layers of hydrogen and helium that become increasingly dense under extreme pressure.

Outer Atmosphere

The visible cloud layers contain hydrogen, helium, ammonia, and other atmospheric compounds.

Metallic Hydrogen

Deeper inside, pressure becomes so intense that hydrogen can enter a metallic, electrically conducting state.

Central Core Region

Scientists believe Saturn contains a dense central region with heavier elements, possibly spread through a diffuse core.

A REMARKABLE SATURN FEATURE

The Magnificent Rings

A System of Billions of Particles

Saturn's rings are made up of countless individual particles, ranging from tiny grains of dust to large chunks of ice. Most of the material is water ice.

The rings are divided into several major sections, labelled with letters such as A, B, and C. Gaps between the rings are shaped partly by the gravitational influence of Saturn's moons.

EXPLORING THE RINGED PLANET

Saturn Missions

C

Cassini-Huygens Mission

The Cassini spacecraft arrived at Saturn in 2004 and spent more than 13 years studying the planet, its rings, and its moons.

Its Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005, becoming the first spacecraft to land on a moon in the outer Solar System. Cassini's mission ended in 2017 when the spacecraft entered Saturn's atmosphere.
