GET TO KNOW THE PLANET

Explore Jupiter

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in the Solar System. It is so massive that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it by volume.

Unlike Earth, Jupiter does not have a solid surface that spacecraft can land on. It is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, with thick layers of clouds and an atmosphere that becomes denser deeper inside.

Jupiter also has a powerful magnetic field and a large system of rings. Its many moons include Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, and Io, each with its own unusual features.

Did you know?

Jupiter rotates faster than any other planet in the Solar System, completing one rotation in about 9.9 hours.

Jupiter's atmosphere contains many colourful cloud bands and enormous storms.

WHAT IS INSIDE JUPITER?

Internal Structure

Jupiter does not have a solid surface like Earth. Its interior contains layers of hydrogen and helium under extremely high pressure and temperature.

Outer Atmosphere

The visible cloud layers contain ammonia, water, and other compounds.

Metallic Hydrogen

Deep inside, extreme pressure transforms hydrogen into an electrically conducting state.

Central Core Region

Scientists think Jupiter has a dense central region containing heavier elements, possibly mixed with surrounding hydrogen.

A REMARKABLE JUPITER FEATURE

The Great Red Spot

A Giant Planetary Storm

The Great Red Spot is a gigantic storm in Jupiter's atmosphere. It is an enormous anticyclonic storm that has been observed for centuries.

Its reddish colour and changing size make it one of the most recognisable features in the Solar System. The storm is large enough to contain Earth, although its size changes over time.

EXPLORING THE GAS GIANT

Jupiter Missions

J

Juno Spacecraft

NASA's Juno spacecraft arrived at Jupiter in July 2016. It studies the planet's atmosphere, magnetic field, gravity, and internal structure.

Its observations help scientists understand how Jupiter formed and how giant planets develop. Juno also takes detailed images of Jupiter and some of its moons.
